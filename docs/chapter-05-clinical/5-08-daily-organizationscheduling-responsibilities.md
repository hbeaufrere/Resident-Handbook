# 5.8 Daily Organization/Scheduling Responsibilities

1.  Look at the appointment schedule well in advance so that you are prepared for cases that will be presented during your time on clinics. The weekly schedule should be evaluated every day, and any cases of concern or referrals should be addressed as far in advance as possible. **It is your responsibility to review the receiving schedule in advance for referrals so that the case can be investigated ahead of the scheduled appointment and have appropriate documentation to support their medical/diagnostic plans. The schedules for advanced imaging (especially CT) tend to fill up 2-3 weeks in advance, so be sure to monitor your schedule and reserve imaging slots for upcoming patients when appropriate. Technicians can also assist in this task. If the case was directly referred through faculty or coordinator, these procedures may have been scheduled when the appointment was made, but this is not always the case.** The house officer receiving the referral case must contact the service coordinator and/or the referring veterinarian and attempt to get as much documentation ahead of time as possible so that the medical records can be reviewed by the resident, faculty and student on the case. If the case is scheduled for an inappropriate day, as discussed by the faculty & resident working the case together, then you should contact the service coordinator or the client to have it re-scheduled. Depending on the nature of the case, you should contact the client ahead of time (especially if the client is traveling a great distance) so that the client is prepared for their appointment. This thoroughness will allow you to best prepare the client and to schedule the referral case appropriately (i.e. advanced/complicated workup).

2.  Schedule and organize procedures for your patients. Faculty and technicians may help with these responsibilities, however, you must follow-up to ensure needs were communicated correctly. You must organize your day so that all procedures are efficiently scheduled and so you can complete duties of importance in a timely manner (i.e. calling back referring veterinarians, contacting clients, having procedures scheduled based on technical staff’s working hours). The faculty on clinics can often provide their schedule which will be filled with administrative meetings, etc. If the faculty wants to be present for a procedure, please consider that when setting up procedures or appointments with consulting services.

3.  Schedule procedures on the service calendar in Outlook as far in advance as possible, in consultation with the Technician supervisor, Cristin Dietrich, and faculty that will be on clinics with you to ensure there is adequate time available. This includes the patient number (if available), patient name and species, procedure, procedure time (if available), and clinician. Procedures yet to be confirmed should be placed on the calendar with the word “tentative”. Verbal communication with the technicians is also necessary in addition to scheduling on the calendar. **You must communicate with the faculty and house officer who will be on clinics to review the case prior to scheduling the procedure.** This is to ensure faculty is comfortable with the plan and procedure, and because faculty often have complex referral cases they are trying to fit in.

4.  Prior to performing any procedure (avoid same day planning whenever possible), make sure that all equipment you would like to use is ready for the procedure in advance of the procedure. **There is a checklist that should be filled out and provided to the technicians in advance of the procedure**. In many cases, discussions with the technicians regarding specialized equipment for the procedure (in addition to the checklist) may also be necessary. If in doubt, discuss it with the technicians and consult with faculty if unsure about specific equipment.

5.  Coordinate and communicate with **all** services involved with a procedure and ensure that everyone involved in the case has the appropriate documentation/background information prior to the procedure. For example, if the surgery and anesthesia services are involved in a procedure, it is your responsibility with faculty to ensure that the anesthesia protocol is planned together, that appropriate anatomy and physiology and specialized equipment have been discussed with the surgeons before the procedure. Be sure you are knowledgeable of the anatomy for each planned procedure.

6.  Anesthesia requires that a case be scheduled in the computer **no later than 3:00 PM the day prior** to the procedure, including scheduling on a Sunday, for a Monday procedure. Requests turned in later may be charged an emergency anesthesia fee, even though it may not be a medical/surgical emergency. If you cancel a procedure it is your responsibility to notify or delegate notification to all involved services as soon as possible.
