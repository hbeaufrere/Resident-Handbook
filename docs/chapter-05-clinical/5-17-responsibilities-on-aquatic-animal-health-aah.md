# 5.17 Responsibilities on Aquatic Animal Health (AAH)

Dr. Esteban Soto is the faculty member responsible for clinical activities of the AAH service. There is a veterinarian in a 1-year AAH fellowship with Dr Soto and CEAMS. This fellow will be present from August through January and has numerous responsibilities, including assisting with the CEAMS/AAH clinical appointments. The receiving line 1 CEAMS faculty member is responsible for AAH decisions in the absence of Dr Soto and the AAH fellow; unless otherwise arranged by Dr. Soto.

AAH is dedicated to preventative, diagnostic, primary, and tertiary veterinary care for aquatic animals. Clients for this service may include but not be limited to individual owners, wholesale/retailers, California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW), commercial aquaculture, public aquariums, zoos, and research entities including support for UC Davis Laboratory Animal Medicine.

Fridays (starting at 10AM; unless field visits are programmed) are reserved for AAH appointments and training opportunities with the following exceptions:

1.  If the Aquatic Animal Health schedule has not been filled by **5pm** on the Tuesday of the same week, CEAMS faculty will decide appointments, tasks or other learning opportunities for resident on the Thursday.

The reason for the deadline is to allow the house officer scheduled to be on Receiving Line 1 for the upcoming Friday AAH day, to inform reception to open Friday appointments to schedule CEAMS patient appointments, and to allow enough time for the house officer and technician to prepare for the Friday appointment. Discussion with AAH faculty/fellow should occur at the latest on the Wednesday afternoon prior to the Friday meeting. This is an expectation that the house officer for the upcoming receiving line 1 must follow through by the Wednesday prior to their AAH Friday.

AAH field calls are scheduled as needed. Work for this service may take place in the CEAMS clinic, AAH laboratories in Tupper Hall, or at the wet lab facilities at the Center for Aquatic Biology and Aquaculture (CABA). Clients for this service may also submit samples via mail Monday-Friday and house officer involvement for these cases will be based on clinic scheduling, case appropriateness, and faculty approval, unless these are sent for Friday processing, in which case the house officer receiving line 1 is expected to work the case as any other appointment. House officers may be required to perform necropsies (even after hours) on this service. The objective of these cases are to provide the best veterinary care for aquatic animals, advance veterinary knowledge in AAH, and to teach house officers how to approach and manage these unique cases.

Outpatient appointments in AAH are generally a minimum of two hours in duration and physical examinations and many diagnostic tests may be performed in front of the client. All patients are anesthetized for examinations. Initially the faculty clinician on this service will accompany the resident on all appointments and be involved in all cases, but as his/her experience increases cases will become the responsibility of the house officer with faculty oversight.

Many patients belong to regular clients, so house officers are required to have a thorough knowledge of these cases before commencing an assigned case. Required reading for this service involves material found on CANVAS, in the CEAMS library, or material provided by Dr. Soto or other AAH staff (fellow or staff vet). Additional materials can be requested from CEAMS faculty in some cases. These materials and cases should be reviewed before commencing an assigned clinics week.

Efficiency in history taking, physical examination and client communication is imperative to ensure that the service runs on time. Student involvement may be minimal on this service at times, so the house officer will be responsible for all aspects of patient/client care and record writing. House officer must complete the AAH request form for technicians to prepare items for onsite and field calls (AAH checklist) at least 48h prior to the case to allow technicians to prepare all equipment needed. Additionally, house officers need to familiarize with the case and communicate with Dr. Soto at least 24h prior to appointment (ideally by Wednesday prior to the Friday appointment) to generate the estimate to be provided and agreed by the client. Records for appointments of any given week must be completed by the close of Wednesday of the following week. Some records on this service will be more involved than CEAMS cases since they may be used as teaching materials, and clients will also provide extensive histories and images of their animals, life support systems (LSS), and aquatic habitats. Many clients communicate via email and this is an acceptable way to exchange information, report water quality results, provide more detailed discharge instructions, etc. **Please cc faculty on all communications.** The house officer retains all follow-up communication on cases seen on AAH even past their assigned weeks on this service. For example, test results that are received for a patient after the house officer’s weeks on AAH, remain the responsibility of that house officer for follow-up. If results are still pending prior to vacations or offsite clinical weeks, the house officer must make the faculty aware of pending test results and the house officer should include these cases in the case transfer list. The house officer will also be responsible for responding to any telephone or email inquiries received, and may be asked to consult for any AAH emergencies received during this time period.

If CEAMS fish patients die and are submitted for necropsy, the default should be to submit to the VMTH pathology service. If clients or potential clients are interested in necropsies of non-CEAMS fish patients, these animals should be submitted to the The California Animal Health and Food Safety Laboratory (<https://cahfs.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/>) or to the Comparative Pathology Laboratory (<https://cpl.ucdavis.edu/>). CEAMS does not accept carcasses of non-patients.
