# 5.6 In Patient Care Responsibilities

1.  Assess your hospitalized patients twice daily (before AM rounds and evenings) every day (except on weekends when you are not on call and your inpatients are transferred to the on call weekend resident).

2.  You must be present during morning and evening treatments, including weekend treatments (when you are on call for that weekend). Client-owned animals must be assessed by a clinician twice daily. If there are no client/zoo owned patients in the hospital, you do not need to be present for evening treatments if you feel comfortable with technician / student capabilities. The exception to this is when fractious wildlife cases (eagles, mammals, etc) are in the hospital.

3.  Treatments must be completed by 8AM on weekdays except should be done by 7:45AM on Tuesday to be present for Journal Club. It is your responsibility to meet with the students and techs the night before to establish a starting time for treatments. The techs need to know as early as possible to ensure treatment crew students are aware of the change in schedule Monday--Friday. Weekend treatment times are 8:00AM and 5:00 PM.

4.  Instinct treatment orders are filled out and cover 10AM through 10AM. New orders for in patients must be approved by Dr before 10AM daily**.**

5.  Plans and Instinct Orders for cases to be managed over a weekend by another resident are prepared (including Saturday, Sunday and Monday) by **Friday** afternoon/evening. In addition, individuals responsible for client communication (weekend resident on-call or student) should be identified and made aware of their responsibilities. This is an important aspect of teamwork and will make for smooth transitions of cases and decrease the workload on the resident on call.

### CEAMS Inpatients housed in the CEAMS ward

1.  For patients hospitalized in the CEAMS wards that require ward technicians to evaluate them or provide treatments after hours, orders can be no more frequent than q4 hours. Ward technicians will only check on patients that have a formal Instinct order for a patient observation listed (they will not perform general check-ins that are not formally ordered).

2.  Patients must be added to the whiteboard in the hallway upstairs

3.  CPR status must be listed in Instinct for all patients admitted to the hospital.

### ICU or ICW admission of CEAMS patients

4.  **Communication:** Notify the relevant patient care team (ICU/ICW) as early as possible that you have a patient you would like to admit, ideally before 5:00 PM. Initial notification can be made by a technician, student, or clinician before bringing a patient up, and should include the cage size required (or whether a cage in the care unit will be used). The CEAMS clinician must directly communicate with the patient care team before or at the time of admission to ensure complete understanding of the care required.

5.  **Instinct orders:** Treatment frequency is limited by the level of hospitalization (ICW or ICU); no order can be more frequent than q1 hour in any area of the hospital. Write specific contingencies for each monitored parameter (e.g., for heart rate, list the low and high HR values that should trigger notification in the order comments). Provide a CPR form with code status and appropriate drug doses for every patient admitted to ICW or ICU.

6.  **Clinician contact details:** Ensure the correct name and phone number for the on-call clinician is listed under “Belongings/Notes” on the left-hand section of the patient’s Instinct treatment sheet. If the on-call clinician cannot be reached, the default is to call CEAMS faculty for assistance.

7.  **Medications and equipment:** All equipment and medications required for patient care must be obtained and delivered to the ward where the patient is hospitalized.

8.  **Medical emergencies:** When a CEAMS patient is admitted to ICW, a taxa-specific emergency box will be placed in the Small Animal Emergency Room (SAER); its presence signals to the ER team that a CEAMS patient is in the wards (no rounding of the ER clinical team will occur for these patients). If a CEAMS patient decompensates while in ICW, it will be taken to the SAER for evaluation using the emergency box, and the ER team will provide what care they can until the CEAMS clinician arrives.

9.  **Special circumstances:** Several ICU team members have severe allergies to rabbits (and possibly guinea pigs), so these species may not be admissible to ICU when those individuals are on duty. ICU and ICW also close intermittently to new admissions when full. A CEAMS clinician will need to remain with the patient to provide care when: (1) constant monitoring or treatments more frequent than q1 hour are required; (2) ICU-level care (e.g., q1 hour treatments) is needed but ICU is not available; or (3) overnight care is required and both ICW and ICU are closed.

10. **CEAMS clinician staffing for special circumstances:** When a CEAMS clinician must stay with a patient, those requiring a high level or frequency of care that cannot be admitted to ICU will be housed in ICW with treatments primarily managed by the CEAMS clinician; patients requiring lower-frequency care may be managed either in ICW or CEAMS. ICW technicians may secondarily assist (including with two-person treatments, working around their existing duties) but are not primarily responsible. Overnight care planning is performed by the CEAMS faculty on the case in consultation with ICU faculty and VMTH leadership; CEAMS faculty (on clinics) and house officers (on and off clinics) will split evening coverage between at least two clinicians when possible. Students on the CEAMS rotation may be asked to come in overnight to assist during high-volume treatment times, at the discretion of the CEAMS faculty on clinics.
