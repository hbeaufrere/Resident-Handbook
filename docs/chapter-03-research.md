# 3. Research Responsibilities — Residents

## 3.1 Scientific Publications and Presentations

Residents must be the first author on at least three (3) publications in the field of zoological medicine in peer-reviewed journals. No more than two (2) of the publications can be case reports or case series. Be mindful that case reports must meet specific, stringent criteria and are often rejected by ACZM. One publication must be an original investigation, but we recommend and provide opportunities for 3 studies (including case series) to be guaranteed to be ACZM eligible on time. The deadline for ACZM eligibility is typically at the end of March of your final year. See ACZM website for specific criteria of publication acceptance by ACZM Credentialing Committee. Any need for clarification can be directed to your faculty mentor.

Each potential publication must be discussed and pre-approved by your faculty mentor. When you identify a project or a clinical case for publication, please discuss the idea with faculty directly involved with the clinical case or the research and also with your primary mentor. The primary mentor has an overview of all priorities relative to your residency and can assist in coordination with other publications and presentations with all faculty.

Ideally each resident project and publication will be mentored by a different faculty member to give the resident an opportunity to work closely with all faculty. This is a system to ensure that each resident has the benefit of being mentored in project design and manuscript writing by multiple faculty members. Alternatively, it also ensures that each faculty member has an opportunity to work closely on a project with every resident. It is expected that at a minimum the facilitator (faculty) will be a co-author on any associated publication. Research projects can originate with faculty members outside the service but need to have a UC Davis affiliation and must be discussed with your faculty mentor in advance.

**Research projects or case reports that the resident initiated prior to the residency but are incomplete at the start of the residency require approval from faculty if time is needed during the residency to complete them.** Priority will be given to those initiated during the residency at UC Davis. If approved the external projects must be submitted in the early part of the residency, **before the end of the first year**. Your primary mentor will assist with this process.

The resident research projects should be written on the white board in the conference room and should be kept up to date with the names of your projects, faculty involved, and expected submit/publish dates.

All research and case material developed during the residency program is the property of UC Davis. You will be expected to leave laboratory notebooks if used and all collected data with the mentor or facilitator of the project. You can take photocopies of the data with you but not the original lab book if used. See Appendix II for how to organize a laboratory book.

The resident has **one year after finalization of the residency program to complete** any ongoing or pending publications. After that period, if needed, dependent on the progress made towards completion of the manuscript, the faculty might decide to withdraw the right to first authorship in order to finalize it.

## 3.2 Original Research

The goals of the resident’s research endeavors are to obtain experience:

1)  with experimental design with defined hypothesis, objectives, statistical analysis, and data interpretation.

2)  in grant preparation/submission and scientific writing.

3)  presenting research results at scientific conferences.

4)  producing manuscripts for peer review publication.

Each resident is expected to complete at least one primary research project originating from UC Davis during the residency program. It is a requirement of the residency to complete a research project and present your original data at the UCD House Officers Seminar Day no later than the spring of the final year of your residency. It is a requirement for ACZM credentialing and for successful completion of the UC Davis residency that your original research manuscript be submitted to and accepted by a peer-reviewed journal according to [ACZM publication requirements](https://www.aczm.org/content.aspx?page_id=22&club_id=366916&module_id=49019).

You are **required to write and submit a grant** for a project. Identify a project that interests you **as soon as you can and discuss with your primary mentor***.* The first opportunity comes from the Center for Companion Animal Health (CCAH) resident grants (these are internal grants, <https://ccah.vetmed.ucdavis.edu>) with a October/November deadline of **your first year***.* Additional funding can be pursued through other granting agencies (see Appendix V).

When planning a research project, take into consideration the restricted time blocks available to residents (i.e. 1-2 week blocks of time for data collection). The research project should be **submitted for publication by the end of the first year** and the expectation is for the resident to present the results at a national or international conference such as AAZV, ExoticsCon/Exotic365, ICARE, AAV, ARAV, AEMV, IAAAM, etc, in addition to the UCD House Officers Seminar Day. See Appendix V for abstract submission deadlines.

## 3.3 Case Reports

Each resident is expected to identify potential case reports or case series that fulfill the ACZM criteria for acceptance in your credentials application from the CEAMS and AAH Services or external rotations. Discuss each opportunity with your primary mentor.
