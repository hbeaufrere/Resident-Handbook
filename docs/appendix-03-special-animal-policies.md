# Appendix III. Special Animal Policies

### CEAMS DOES NOT SEE:

*<u>Most Adult Ratites</u>*

At faculty discretion depending on weight. If we cannot see it, advise the client on where to call. Check our referral Veterinarian list. Dr Brian Speer is the one that we refer to most.

*<u>Wolf and First-Generation Wolf-hybrids</u>*

Calls regarding these patients should be referred to Dr. Gjeltema and the Zoological Medicine Service. These patients are seen through their service only per clinician discretion.

*<u>Primates</u>*

If you receive a primate call, please contact your faculty to discuss. In some cases we do see small primates.

*<u>Small Exotic Cat Hybrids</u>*

We do not see either canine or feline hybrids

### CEAMS WILL SEE:

*<u>Ferrets, hedgehogs, sugar gliders, small exotic cats and other species requiring permits</u>*

These pets are illegal to own in the state of California, however current California law enables us to treat these patients in the clinic. Some organizations will have permits from California Fish & Wildlife for education & display and these should be requested at the appointment*<u>.</u>*

*<u>Rock pigeons</u>*

Rock pigeons are non-native and have a significant likelihood of carrying infectious agents that are communicable to other avian species, and there is a risk of zoonosis. Rock pigeons can be seen by our service per faculty discretion. It is not common to see band-tailed pigeons, but these birds are native wildlife, threatened in CA and are sometimes presented for evaluation.

Stray, banded pigeons shall be maintained by the service for 6 days. The service shall make a reasonable effort during that time to contact the owner of the bird. After 6 days the animal will be transferred to the SPCA or humanely euthanized. Please make sure the animal's band number is recorded in the chart and is transferred with the pigeon if it goes to the SPCA.

*<u>Pet Raccoons, Skunks & Opossums</u>*

These animals can be seen by the Service, per clinician discretion. Wild raccoons, skunks, and opossums are not seen.

*<u>Deer</u>*

We occasionally see wild deer for humane euthanasia only. These animals are usually brought in by animal control. We do not have the facilities to house and properly care for these animals. We refer other inquiries to California Fish and Game as necessary.

*<u>Bats</u>*

Because of the high incidence of rabies in bats, CEAMS personnel will euthanize these animals as needed with staff wearing appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE).*

MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING

CARE OF WILD HYBRID PETS IN THE SMALL ANIMAL CLINIC

Date: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The University of California, Davis Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital (VMTH) considers the following small animals as wild hybrids: Bengal cats (Asian leopard cat crosses), Savannah cats (Serval crosses), bobcat or jungal cat hybrids; wolf, dingo or coyote hybrids; any other mix of a wild dog or cat species with a domestic dog or cat, respectively.

Under California State Law, possession of a wild hybrid cat is permitted, but possession of a wolf or a first generation hybrid is not. The VMTH will examine and treat wild hybrid cats and dogs. However, wild hybrids are considered unvaccinated for rabies regardless of their rabies vaccination status, because there is no vaccine approved for hybrid species. If an unvaccinated hybrid animal bites a human, public health officials will consider the animal wild and may 1) request immediate humane euthanasia of that animal so that it can be tested for rabies or 2) request prolonged quarantine at the owner’s expense, depending on the circumstances. The VMTH is required by law to comply with any decision made by public health officials. The VMTH takes all possible precautions to prevent bite/scratch incidents. However, even in the most controlled environments a bite/scratch incident may be unpreventable, and all bite/scratch wounds must be reported to public health officials. Therefore, as an owner of a wild hybrid, you are required to sign this memorandum of understanding *before* we examine and treat your pet.

I certify that I am the owner or authorized agent for the owner of \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (pet/animal’s name or ID). I understand that bite/scratch incidents are not always preventable and that the VMTH must report bite/scratch incidents to public health officials by law. I acknowledge that because my pet/animal(s) is considered a wild hybrid, public health officials *may* insist on euthanasia and testing for rabies or a lengthy (e.g., 30 day) quarantine at my expense should a bite/scratch incident occur, depending on the circumstances (the normal procedure for an unvaccinated domestic dog or cat that bites a human would be quarantine for 10 days).

**FERRET HEALTH IN THE COMPANION EXOTIC ANIMAL MEDICINE AND SURGERY SERVICE**

UC Davis Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital

California law allows veterinarians to treat ferrets even though ferret ownership is unlawful in the state of California. However, the law requires veterinarians, including the UC Davis Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital (VMTH), to report **any animal bite** to appropriate animal control agencies. If your animal ever bites during a visit to the VMTH, we must report the bite to the appropriate animal control agency, as we do all other animal bites. If this occurs, the animal control agency may quarantine your pet.

Faculty and staff do everything possible to minimize potential problems such as animal bites during your pet's visit.

We look forward to providing the best care available for your ferret.
