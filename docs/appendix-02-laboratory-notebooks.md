# Appendix II. Policy and Procedures Related to Completion of Laboratory Notebooks

**I. Purpose**

The purpose of this document is to provide guidance on the use and maintenance of laboratory notebooks for laboratory activities. For the purpose of this document, “laboratory notebook” is a bound collection of serially numbered pages used to record the planning and progress of scientific investigation. Adherence to the policy and procedures will ensure that ideas and experimental information are maintained accurately and consistently, adequately support patents rights, provide witness corroboration, and prevent loss or theft of the same.

At the faculty discretion, the laboratory notebook may be in an electronic format.

3.  **Maintaining Laboratory Notebooks**

<!-- -->

1.  **Procedures**

The laboratory notebook is the primary record of planning, purpose, and experimental work. It represents a daily record and is the vehicle required to document laboratory experimental work indicating daily purpose and conclusions as well as data and observations. It the first place to record all data, observations and anything else directly pertaining to the study.

The main purposes of maintaining a laboratory notebook are to preserve experimental planning, protocol development, conduct of study, data and observations, conclusions and recommendations of future direction for all research and development activity in a form that can be understood and reproduced by another scientist. This is done so the purpose and planning of the study is understood and studies or research projects may be reconstructed, repeated, and defended.

Quality control activities (e.g., certificates of analysis) and practices related to equipment maintenance and calibration are recorded on the appropriate forms and maintained in log books. It is not necessary to record these activities in the laboratory notebook though these forms should be referred to and cross-referenced in the laboratory notebook.

There should be a separate Laboratory Notebook for each study.

2.  **Laboratory Notebook Format:**

<u>Your research mentor will provide you with a Laboratory Notebook.</u>

<u>Title page:</u> When beginning a new Laboratory Notebook, the Scientist should record on page 1 the project title and the names and contacts of the primary investigator(s) and research associates.

<u>Table of contents:</u> This a running list to keep track of different phases and aspects of the project - to be added as needed.

For each experiment undertaken the following sections, listed as subheadings, should be clearly present:

**<u>Experimental descriptions:</u>** Separate experiments using clearly stated descriptions. List the experiment in the Table of Contents.

**<u>Experiment purpose and objective (rationale):</u>** The Scientist should document the daily plan of the experiment before initiating the lab work. Record in precise language the question being addressed by the study and the general plan or experimental design. Changes in plan should be documented.

The Scientist should note the date when each experiment is started. If work carries over more than one day, each succeeding day’s entry should note the date of same. The record of an experiment that takes more than one page should make definite references to previous and following pages so it can be followed, for example, “go to p.\_” and “from p. \_”.

References that are relevant to the inception of the work pursued should be clearly cited.

<u>**Materials and Methods**:</u> All materials used in the study should be noted including source and lot number, calculations, yields, characterizing data, and so on. The method or procedure should be fully described including equipment used, conditions, times, and temperatures where applicable. If a standard or routine procedure is being followed, the current version should be contained in the Laboratory Notebook and future experiments in that Laboratory Notebook should reference its location. Any modifications to the referenced procedure should be noted. References that are relevant to the materials and methods employed should be clearly cited (For example when a specific published technique is used, the complete reference should be included in this section).

<u>**Experimental Results**:</u> Whether data supports, refutes, or has no apparent value to the stated experimental objective, all outcomes from a study should be recorded in the Laboratory Notebook.

Any results in the form of graphs, drawings, or printouts should be affixed in the Laboratory Notebook using a permanent method such as glue or tape. Reference should be made to the affixed material on the bound page and the Scientist should initial and date over the interface.

If computer software is used, to generate or collect data, the name of the program and the version number should be documented.

Results that are numerical should be written, or printed and affixed, into the record and should include raw data in addition to any numerical data analysis. All columns and rows should be labeled to describe the data set contained therein.

Results that are image-based should be printed and attached into the record. They should include a legend describing each image or refer to a table that fully describes the test condition for which the outcome represents. The file path for the saved image file should be provided with the data.

Results that are in graph format should be attached into the record. The graph should contain labels for the test conditions that clearly and concisely define the parameters. The x and y axes should concisely describe the test groups, the result being measured, and the units of measurement. The legend key should describe the data points represented by each icon.

Any information that is affixed into the notebook, such as product inserts or printed graphs, should also be copied and affixed onto the duplicate page of the Laboratory Notebook.

**<u>Conclusions and Future Work:</u>** The record should contain an evaluation of the meaning or impact of the results on the project and a short discussion of the future direction of study based on the latest experiment or series of experiments.

For each new invention the following sections, listed as subheadings, should be clearly present:

> **<u>Title of Invention:</u>** Use concise language to clearly describe the idea.

**<u>Concept:</u>** Record in precise language the idea being presented in the invention. Describe a problem that can be alleviated through the use of this invention or why current methods are inadequate. Describe what is novel about the invention and provide broad statements of the invention.

**<u>Reduction to Practice:</u>** Describe how the idea could be implemented, using sketches, photographs or data where appropriate. The specific, preferred practice of the invention should be set forth as well as alternative approaches.

> **<u>Inventor(s):</u>** Name all employees who creatively contributed to the invention.

**<u>Date First Articulated:</u>** Provide the date that the idea was first described and obtain signatures of all inventors. In addition, two individuals capable of understanding the technology should review, sign, and date the written disclosure as witnesses.

The following are general guidelines for documenting work in the Laboratory Notebook.

> All entries in the Laboratory Notebook should be completed in permanent ink. **Do not use pencil.**
>
> Experiments should be recorded in chronological order.
>
> <u>Errors should be crossed through with a single line and initialed and dated.</u> If the error is more than a few words, an explanation for the error should be noted in the margin where the error is corrected.
>
> The Laboratory Notebook should be available in the lab while conducting a study or research. Don’t rely on memory.
>
> All numerical data should be captured directly in the notebook. Data from instruments that are interfaced to computers should be printed electronically and the printout affixed in the notebook. Data manually captured should be entered into a pre-configured, printed template whenever possible. For purposes of analyzing the data the numbers can be entered into a spreadsheet but the original data **must** be affixed in the notebook. **Do not transcribe** the data into a notebook; rather affix the original document.
>
> All essential facts should be recorded. Abbreviations and codes should be chosen and used in an unambiguous and consistent way. Non-standard abbreviations and codes should be defined in the Laboratory Notebook before using same.
>
> In general, only one subject should be recorded on each page. Long Term projects should have separate books. All projects and experiments should be recorded in such a manner that any co-worker may continue the operation in the Scientist’s absence or reassignment.A single diagonal line should be drawn across any unused portion of a page and it should be initialed and dated to forestall any challenge that the record has been augmented at a later date.

**C. Witness Corroboration:**

When the record is complete, or when there will be some delay before the work will proceed, the Laboratory Notebook page should be witnessed promptly by a Scientist who is NOT an immediate member/contributor of the project team. The witness should examine the notebook records for adherence to the notebook policy, then sign and date each page. This corroborates the existence of the record at the date of signing.

Witnessing should be done no more than 2 weeks after the entries are made.

Names of Operators and Witnesses who are present during a demonstration should be recorded. At least one witness, not claiming to be a co-inventor, should sign and date in the space provided at the bottom of the entry in the Laboratory Notebook. New concepts and new solutions to problems should be witnessed by your co-worker or someone competent to understand the material being recorded. This fact should also be recorded, signed and dated.

New ideas, plans, procedures, sketches, etc. should be recorded immediately in the Laboratory Notebook at the time they occur. These should be **disclosed to, and understood by co-workers who sign and date this fact.**

When an experiment shows results of possible patentable importance, and no witnesses are present, the procedure should be repeated under supervision by co-workers as soon as possible. Data covering the experiment should be recorded in both the Scientist’s and the co-worker’s Laboratory Notebook with proper signatures and dates.

**IV. Procedures for Securing Laboratory Notebooks**

**A.** Control the location of and access to the Laboratory Notebook.

**B. All Employees, Consultants and Students acknowledge that the Laboratory Notebooks and all information recorded therein are the exclusive property of University of California, Davis. All Employees, Consultants and Students further acknowledge that all contents of the Laboratory Notebooks are to be held strictly confidential and <u>must be</u> turned in upon completion, upon request or upon termination for whatever reason. The person who has custody of a Laboratory Notebook must take every precaution to safeguard it against loss, damage or destruction. In case of fire, theft, damage or disappearance of a Laboratory Notebook, the Employee, Consultant or Student will immediately notify the Office Administrator, or in the case that the Office Administrator has such knowledge, his or her Supervisor. A written report describing the circumstances of the loss, damage or destruction should follow.**
