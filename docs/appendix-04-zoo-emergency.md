# Appendix IV. Zoo Medicine Service Emergency Policy

**The Zoo Medicine Service does NOT offer an Emergency Service.**

The Zoo Medicine Service through the School of Veterinary Medicine at UC Davis is different from other traditional services at the VMTH. The Zoological Medicine Service is based out of the Wildlife Health Center and the Sacramento Zoo providing didactic as well as clinical instruction for veterinary students and graduate veterinarians (residents). The service does not have dedicated ward space in the VMTH nor does it have assigned technical staff. The VMTH is not designed to house large non-domestic animals. The clinical service and instruction is provided primarily through the administration of service contracts with specific institutions.

The Zoo Medicine Service currently consists of Dr. Jenessa Gjeltema. Dr. Gjeltema does not have scheduled clinical time at the VMTH; clinical service is primarily scheduled at the Sacramento Zoo through service contracts. Dr. Gjeltema welcomes consultation calls from referring practitioners but is very limited in what cases can be seen at the VMTH. Dr. Gjeltema will facilitate the referral of zoological medicine cases from outside zoological institutions requiring VMTH outpatient-based diagnostics or therapy. These appointments are generally booked well in advance due to the multiple service schedules which must be coordinated. Due to the lack of available resources (clinician time, ward space, technical support) the service does not routinely treat privately-owned animals.

The Zoo Med Service is currently staffed Dr. Gjeltema and a first year Zoo Med Resident. The Service does not routinely hospitalize patients so its ability to receive emergencies is very limited. As a result, animals owned by the general public are not routinely seen by the Zoo Med Service on an emergency basis. The only emergencies that will be seen are where there is a zoological medicine clinician available, and a referring veterinarian who can take over the outpatient case upon release from the VMTH. In general, the Zoo Med Service does not make field calls on an emergency basis. **Dr. Gjeltema (not the resident) on duty must OK seeing the emergency client.**

**NEVER tell the client to bring the patient to the hospital**. Please contact a clinician according to the flow chart. The clinician will determine whether the patient can be seen, give the client instructions and let admissions know what is happening.

8)  The Zoo Med Service sometimes can field calls for information or consultation **on cases concerning non-pet, non-domestic animals. When calls are received, they should be forwarded to Dr.** Gjeltema **who will, in consultation with the first year Zoo Med Resident, provide information to the client or veterinarian.**

If it is an INFORMATION CALL, please forward the call with contact information to a zoological medicine clinician in the following order:

1.  Dr. Jenessa Gjeltema 916-808-8812

2.  First year zoological medicine resident

**Even if you ARE successful in contacting one of the zoo med veterinarians, the caller will typically be asked to leave a message so that the clinicians can address the information call at a later time. We will attempt to address all information calls within 24 hours, but please let people know that it could take longer.**

**The service sees very few privately owned animals at the VMTH and will only sometimes provide phone consultations. The service schedules very few appointments at the VMTH.**
