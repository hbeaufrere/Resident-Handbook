# 5. Clinical Responsibilities

## 5.1 Professional Relations

1.  It is imperative that all the members within the service be able to interact easily and freely with each other.

2.  A “team approach” is a key component to working with our services. All team members should be willing to perform routine tasks (cleaning, feeding, patient admission, emergency case admission, etc.) to help out others on the team as needed.

3.  It is expected that doctors, technicians, technician assistants, and students participate in cases in a collegial manner and with appropriate professional communication.

4.  Communication between team members is critical to ensure an organized and efficient clinical schedule.

5.  Your same professionalism is expected when working with every other service in the VMTH and during your external rotations. You are a representative of UC Davis while on these rotations, and your professionalism reflects on our entire program.

2.  The communication needs of the case should determine whether the faculty, house officer, or student maintains communication with the client. It is the house officer’s responsibility to ensure that the student meets the communication needs or ask from help from faculty if needed.

## 5.6 In Patient Care Responsibilities

1.  Assess your hospitalized patients twice daily (before AM rounds and evenings) every day (except on weekends when you are not on call and your inpatients are transferred to the on call weekend resident).

2.  You must be present during morning and evening treatments, including weekend treatments (when you are on call for that weekend). Client-owned animals must be assessed by a clinician twice daily. If there are no client/zoo owned patients in the hospital, you do not need to be present for evening treatments if you feel comfortable with technician / student capabilities. The exception to this is when fractious wildlife cases (eagles, mammals, etc) are in the hospital.

3.  Treatments must be completed by 8AM on weekdays except should be done by 7:45AM on Tuesday to be present for Journal Club. It is your responsibility to meet with the students and techs the night before to establish a starting time for treatments. The techs need to know as early as possible to ensure treatment crew students are aware of the change in schedule Monday--Friday. Weekend treatment times are 8:00AM and 5:00 PM.

4.  Instinct treatment orders are filled out and cover 10AM through 10AM. New orders for in patients must be approved by Dr before 10AM daily**.**

5.  Plans and Instinct Orders for cases to be managed over a weekend by another resident are prepared (including Saturday, Sunday and Monday) by **Friday** afternoon/evening. In addition, individuals responsible for client communication (weekend resident on-call or student) should be identified and made aware of their responsibilities. This is an important aspect of teamwork and will make for smooth transitions of cases and decrease the workload on the resident on call.

### CEAMS Inpatients housed in the CEAMS ward

1.  For patients hospitalized in the CEAMS wards that require ward technicians to evaluate them or provide treatments after hours, orders can be no more frequent than q4 hours. Ward technicians will only check on patients that have a formal Instinct order for a patient observation listed (they will not perform general check-ins that are not formally ordered).

2.  Patients must be added to the whiteboard in the hallway upstairs

3.  CPR status must be listed in Instinct for all patients admitted to the hospital.

### ICU or ICW admission of CEAMS patients

4.  **Communication:** Notify the relevant patient care team (ICU/ICW) as early as possible that you have a patient you would like to admit, ideally before 5:00 PM. Initial notification can be made by a technician, student, or clinician before bringing a patient up, and should include the cage size required (or whether a cage in the care unit will be used). The CEAMS clinician must directly communicate with the patient care team before or at the time of admission to ensure complete understanding of the care required.

5.  **Instinct orders:** Treatment frequency is limited by the level of hospitalization (ICW or ICU); no order can be more frequent than q1 hour in any area of the hospital. Write specific contingencies for each monitored parameter (e.g., for heart rate, list the low and high HR values that should trigger notification in the order comments). Provide a CPR form with code status and appropriate drug doses for every patient admitted to ICW or ICU.

6.  **Clinician contact details:** Ensure the correct name and phone number for the on-call clinician is listed under “Belongings/Notes” on the left-hand section of the patient’s Instinct treatment sheet. If the on-call clinician cannot be reached, the default is to call CEAMS faculty for assistance.

7.  **Medications and equipment:** All equipment and medications required for patient care must be obtained and delivered to the ward where the patient is hospitalized.

8.  **Medical emergencies:** When a CEAMS patient is admitted to ICW, a taxa-specific emergency box will be placed in the Small Animal Emergency Room (SAER); its presence signals to the ER team that a CEAMS patient is in the wards (no rounding of the ER clinical team will occur for these patients). If a CEAMS patient decompensates while in ICW, it will be taken to the SAER for evaluation using the emergency box, and the ER team will provide what care they can until the CEAMS clinician arrives.

9.  **Special circumstances:** Several ICU team members have severe allergies to rabbits (and possibly guinea pigs), so these species may not be admissible to ICU when those individuals are on duty. ICU and ICW also close intermittently to new admissions when full. A CEAMS clinician will need to remain with the patient to provide care when: (1) constant monitoring or treatments more frequent than q1 hour are required; (2) ICU-level care (e.g., q1 hour treatments) is needed but ICU is not available; or (3) overnight care is required and both ICW and ICU are closed.

10. **CEAMS clinician staffing for special circumstances:** When a CEAMS clinician must stay with a patient, those requiring a high level or frequency of care that cannot be admitted to ICU will be housed in ICW with treatments primarily managed by the CEAMS clinician; patients requiring lower-frequency care may be managed either in ICW or CEAMS. ICW technicians may secondarily assist (including with two-person treatments, working around their existing duties) but are not primarily responsible. Overnight care planning is performed by the CEAMS faculty on the case in consultation with ICU faculty and VMTH leadership; CEAMS faculty (on clinics) and house officers (on and off clinics) will split evening coverage between at least two clinicians when possible. Students on the CEAMS rotation may be asked to come in overnight to assist during high-volume treatment times, at the discretion of the CEAMS faculty on clinics.

## 5.8 Daily Organization/Scheduling Responsibilities

1.  Look at the appointment schedule well in advance so that you are prepared for cases that will be presented during your time on clinics. The weekly schedule should be evaluated every day, and any cases of concern or referrals should be addressed as far in advance as possible. **It is your responsibility to review the receiving schedule in advance for referrals so that the case can be investigated ahead of the scheduled appointment and have appropriate documentation to support their medical/diagnostic plans. The schedules for advanced imaging (especially CT) tend to fill up 2-3 weeks in advance, so be sure to monitor your schedule and reserve imaging slots for upcoming patients when appropriate. Technicians can also assist in this task. If the case was directly referred through faculty or coordinator, these procedures may have been scheduled when the appointment was made, but this is not always the case.** The house officer receiving the referral case must contact the service coordinator and/or the referring veterinarian and attempt to get as much documentation ahead of time as possible so that the medical records can be reviewed by the resident, faculty and student on the case. If the case is scheduled for an inappropriate day, as discussed by the faculty & resident working the case together, then you should contact the service coordinator or the client to have it re-scheduled. Depending on the nature of the case, you should contact the client ahead of time (especially if the client is traveling a great distance) so that the client is prepared for their appointment. This thoroughness will allow you to best prepare the client and to schedule the referral case appropriately (i.e. advanced/complicated workup).

2.  Schedule and organize procedures for your patients. Faculty and technicians may help with these responsibilities, however, you must follow-up to ensure needs were communicated correctly. You must organize your day so that all procedures are efficiently scheduled and so you can complete duties of importance in a timely manner (i.e. calling back referring veterinarians, contacting clients, having procedures scheduled based on technical staff’s working hours). The faculty on clinics can often provide their schedule which will be filled with administrative meetings, etc. If the faculty wants to be present for a procedure, please consider that when setting up procedures or appointments with consulting services.

3.  Schedule procedures on the service calendar in Outlook as far in advance as possible, in consultation with the Technician supervisor, Cristin Dietrich, and faculty that will be on clinics with you to ensure there is adequate time available. This includes the patient number (if available), patient name and species, procedure, procedure time (if available), and clinician. Procedures yet to be confirmed should be placed on the calendar with the word “tentative”. Verbal communication with the technicians is also necessary in addition to scheduling on the calendar. **You must communicate with the faculty and house officer who will be on clinics to review the case prior to scheduling the procedure.** This is to ensure faculty is comfortable with the plan and procedure, and because faculty often have complex referral cases they are trying to fit in.

4.  Prior to performing any procedure (avoid same day planning whenever possible), make sure that all equipment you would like to use is ready for the procedure in advance of the procedure. **There is a checklist that should be filled out and provided to the technicians in advance of the procedure**. In many cases, discussions with the technicians regarding specialized equipment for the procedure (in addition to the checklist) may also be necessary. If in doubt, discuss it with the technicians and consult with faculty if unsure about specific equipment.

5.  Coordinate and communicate with **all** services involved with a procedure and ensure that everyone involved in the case has the appropriate documentation/background information prior to the procedure. For example, if the surgery and anesthesia services are involved in a procedure, it is your responsibility with faculty to ensure that the anesthesia protocol is planned together, that appropriate anatomy and physiology and specialized equipment have been discussed with the surgeons before the procedure. Be sure you are knowledgeable of the anatomy for each planned procedure.

6.  Anesthesia requires that a case be scheduled in the computer **no later than 3:00 PM the day prior** to the procedure, including scheduling on a Sunday, for a Monday procedure. Requests turned in later may be charged an emergency anesthesia fee, even though it may not be a medical/surgical emergency. If you cancel a procedure it is your responsibility to notify or delegate notification to all involved services as soon as possible.

## 5.10 Responsibilities for Medical Records 

1.  Maintaining accurate and current medical records is mandatory and all in patient records should be reviewed at least once daily. Students are required to have records from the previous day complete by 8AM.

2.  Prepare discharge instructions **<u>in advance</u>** of discharge and get medications ready to go home prior to discharge. If a case is being discharged Monday, the resident should start the discharge instructions and prepare for drugs to go home <u>on Friday.</u>

3.  **All records must be completed and finalized by the primary clinician. There is zero tolerance for records finalized by administration (admin-final, performed 10d after discharge).** Prioritize record completion as 1) Referral cases, 2) Client-owned cases, 3) Wildlife cases. Prioritize cases that other clinicians will be seeing on rechecks even when it is before your 10 day limit.

4.  **You are required to complete records for Client-owned/Aquatic Animal health/CRC/Zoo cases before the end of the week of responsibility (by 8 AM on Saturday morning of the same week)**. Daily faculty/house officer rounds require resident preparation and prompt delivery of all pertinent history, physical examination findings, diagnostic tests performed and results, and appropriate literature searches to discuss diagnostic and therapeutic options.

## 5.11 Rounds

1\. **House officer/Faculty clinical rounds** are daily from 8:00 to 9:00 AM unless precluded by journal club or pathology rounds. The start of the morning rounds may be adjusted based on faculty availability and other commitments.

1.  In most cases, house officers should expect to present the entire case prior to faculty inquiry/input, except when further details or clarification is needed.

2.  To ensure thorough understanding of cases while reducing interruptions, cases should be presented as follows:

    1.  Signalment

    2.  Presenting complaint

    3.  Past pertinent history including historical problems, recent/current medications, and dates/results of recent diagnostic tests

    4.  Relevant husbandry information

    5.  Significant physical examination findings

    6.  Diagnostic test results

    7.  Problem list, with differential diagnoses for active problems

    8.  Current diagnostic and treatment plan

    9.  Long-term plan if indicated

2\. **Student/House officer/Faculty rounds** are Mon-Thurs from 9:00 to 10:00 AM. Friday student rounds at led by technicians from 9-10AM (cadaver practice, technical tips).

a\. Rounds assignments for the week should be determined among faculty and residents before or during resident rounds on Monday morning. In general, each clinician should lead at least one session per week when on clinics. **House officers will present student rounds on Thursdays because the faculty have their weekly meeting.**

b\. **House officers are excused from student rounds during their 2<sup>nd</sup> and 3<sup>rd</sup> years based on faculty discretion if they are not teaching rounds that morning.** It should not be an expectation of the resident to skip student rounds. House officers can be excused to see recheck appointments, make client phone calls, or update medical records during the rounds time if they have demonstrated excellent skills in teaching students in these categories to the satisfaction of the faculty:

1.  Presentation and management of cases

2.  Differential diagnostic lists for common problems

3.  Pathophysiology and presentation of common diseases

4.  Basic clinical pathology

5.  Basic radiology

c\. Case rounds should be guided based on patient signalment, chief presenting complaints, diagnostic procedures, problem list, differential diagnoses and plans. Cases can be helpful to discuss larger topics (e.g. radiology, hematology, etc.) and assess student understanding of their cases.

3.  If there are minimal cases in the hospital, rounds should be case-based oriented when possible (disease processes, radiographs, clinical pathology, etc).

## 5.17 Responsibilities on Aquatic Animal Health (AAH)

Dr. Esteban Soto is the faculty member responsible for clinical activities of the AAH service. There is a veterinarian in a 1-year AAH fellowship with Dr Soto and CEAMS. This fellow will be present from August through January and has numerous responsibilities, including assisting with the CEAMS/AAH clinical appointments. The receiving line 1 CEAMS faculty member is responsible for AAH decisions in the absence of Dr Soto and the AAH fellow; unless otherwise arranged by Dr. Soto.

AAH is dedicated to preventative, diagnostic, primary, and tertiary veterinary care for aquatic animals. Clients for this service may include but not be limited to individual owners, wholesale/retailers, California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW), commercial aquaculture, public aquariums, zoos, and research entities including support for UC Davis Laboratory Animal Medicine.

Fridays (starting at 10AM; unless field visits are programmed) are reserved for AAH appointments and training opportunities with the following exceptions:

1.  If the Aquatic Animal Health schedule has not been filled by **5pm** on the Tuesday of the same week, CEAMS faculty will decide appointments, tasks or other learning opportunities for resident on the Thursday.

The reason for the deadline is to allow the house officer scheduled to be on Receiving Line 1 for the upcoming Friday AAH day, to inform reception to open Friday appointments to schedule CEAMS patient appointments, and to allow enough time for the house officer and technician to prepare for the Friday appointment. Discussion with AAH faculty/fellow should occur at the latest on the Wednesday afternoon prior to the Friday meeting. This is an expectation that the house officer for the upcoming receiving line 1 must follow through by the Wednesday prior to their AAH Friday.

AAH field calls are scheduled as needed. Work for this service may take place in the CEAMS clinic, AAH laboratories in Tupper Hall, or at the wet lab facilities at the Center for Aquatic Biology and Aquaculture (CABA). Clients for this service may also submit samples via mail Monday-Friday and house officer involvement for these cases will be based on clinic scheduling, case appropriateness, and faculty approval, unless these are sent for Friday processing, in which case the house officer receiving line 1 is expected to work the case as any other appointment. House officers may be required to perform necropsies (even after hours) on this service. The objective of these cases are to provide the best veterinary care for aquatic animals, advance veterinary knowledge in AAH, and to teach house officers how to approach and manage these unique cases.

Outpatient appointments in AAH are generally a minimum of two hours in duration and physical examinations and many diagnostic tests may be performed in front of the client. All patients are anesthetized for examinations. Initially the faculty clinician on this service will accompany the resident on all appointments and be involved in all cases, but as his/her experience increases cases will become the responsibility of the house officer with faculty oversight.

Many patients belong to regular clients, so house officers are required to have a thorough knowledge of these cases before commencing an assigned case. Required reading for this service involves material found on CANVAS, in the CEAMS library, or material provided by Dr. Soto or other AAH staff (fellow or staff vet). Additional materials can be requested from CEAMS faculty in some cases. These materials and cases should be reviewed before commencing an assigned clinics week.

Efficiency in history taking, physical examination and client communication is imperative to ensure that the service runs on time. Student involvement may be minimal on this service at times, so the house officer will be responsible for all aspects of patient/client care and record writing. House officer must complete the AAH request form for technicians to prepare items for onsite and field calls (AAH checklist) at least 48h prior to the case to allow technicians to prepare all equipment needed. Additionally, house officers need to familiarize with the case and communicate with Dr. Soto at least 24h prior to appointment (ideally by Wednesday prior to the Friday appointment) to generate the estimate to be provided and agreed by the client. Records for appointments of any given week must be completed by the close of Wednesday of the following week. Some records on this service will be more involved than CEAMS cases since they may be used as teaching materials, and clients will also provide extensive histories and images of their animals, life support systems (LSS), and aquatic habitats. Many clients communicate via email and this is an acceptable way to exchange information, report water quality results, provide more detailed discharge instructions, etc. **Please cc faculty on all communications.** The house officer retains all follow-up communication on cases seen on AAH even past their assigned weeks on this service. For example, test results that are received for a patient after the house officer’s weeks on AAH, remain the responsibility of that house officer for follow-up. If results are still pending prior to vacations or offsite clinical weeks, the house officer must make the faculty aware of pending test results and the house officer should include these cases in the case transfer list. The house officer will also be responsible for responding to any telephone or email inquiries received, and may be asked to consult for any AAH emergencies received during this time period.

If CEAMS fish patients die and are submitted for necropsy, the default should be to submit to the VMTH pathology service. If clients or potential clients are interested in necropsies of non-CEAMS fish patients, these animals should be submitted to the The California Animal Health and Food Safety Laboratory (<https://cahfs.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/>) or to the Comparative Pathology Laboratory (<https://cpl.ucdavis.edu/>). CEAMS does not accept carcasses of non-patients.

## 5.19 Off-Site Rotations - Residents

Several external rotations (or resident externships) are scheduled during the 3 years of the residency program. The purpose of these externships is to provide experiential learning opportunities for Zoological Companion Animal residents to assist in preparation for the ACZM credentialing exam. Over the 3 years of the program, these include 8 weeks at the Sacramento Zoo, 4 weeks at the Oakland Zoo and 2 weeks at the Marine Mammal Center in Sausalito, CA. It is challenging to schedule these externships for 4 individual residents over 3 years so the scheduling is tightly planned. Please be aware that there will be cost of living expenses incurred for these external rotations and these are the resident’s responsibility to budget for those costs, including housing and travel. Whenever possible, the faculty will try to identify a donor or a funding source to assist in covering the costs of these rotations.

## 5.20 Case Transfers When Rotating Off Clinics

When a house officer is on vacation or on an off rotation it is vital that any cases that require follow-up be communicated to the team to ensure timely and accurate follow-up with clients. Before leaving, house officers must

- Complete medical records, especially those that have planned recheck evaluations

- Fill out a case transfer form and email to the service all cases that have outstanding diagnostics or cases where clients are expected to call or need care or advice during your time away

- Email Service Coordinator with your time away so that calls and emails will be diverted and wth any requests for rechecks that need to be scheduled.

House officers must follow up on their own outstanding diagnostics and client follow up questions while on Professional Development.

Raptor center cases: In the absence of rounds the week prior, or if the house officer that will be on CRC that week is not present, ongoing cases of importance should be on the transfer list. The active case list should also be updated to ensure the entire team understands what results are pending or what follow-up is necessary.

Zoo cases: In the absence of rounds the week prior, or if the house officer that will be on MGZ that week is not present, ongoing cases of importance should be on the transfer list. Results that are pending and that are not received BEFORE you go off clinics should be included so the incoming resident knows what to follow up on. The active case list should also be updated to ensure the entire team understands what results are pending or what follow-up is necessary.

All medical records for receiving line 1 cases must be finished prior to rotating off the line of receiving to ensure timely communications with the staff at the facility as well as the resident rotating on to primary that will need to follow-up on specific cases **(by 8 AM Saturday of the same week).**

## 5.21 Moonlighting

Moonlighting is strongly discouraged, as it is not considered compatible with the intensity and time commitments of this training program. On-call responsibilities have been historically reduced to provide house officers with additional time to focus on tasks related to clinical duties, educational development, and scholarly activities. **Furthermore, in the event of illness, it is expected that other house officers will make reasonable efforts to provide coverage for affected emergency shifts.**

## 5.22 Planning ahead for post-program interviews

As you approach the end of your program, you may begin scheduling interviews in preparation for your next professional opportunity. We encourage you to plan these interviews during your allotted vacation or professional development time, ideally toward the end of your program. Please be aware that we may not be able to accommodate additional time off from clinical responsibilities specifically for interviews, so advance planning is essential.
